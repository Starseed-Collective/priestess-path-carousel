# Running this live on a call

Read this once before you start. It takes 5 minutes to set up and about
8 minutes to demo.

## Before the call

**Nothing to install. It is already done on this machine.**

Puppeteer is installed, Chrome is downloaded, and a test slide rendered
successfully. You do not need a terminal and you will not use one.

Two things to have open:

1. **Claude Code, pointed at this folder** (`~/Projects/priestess-path-carousel`).
2. **A Finder window on `demo-photos/`**, so you can show the four real photos
   you are about to use.

If you want to show them how THEY get it, the line to read out is:

> Set up my carousel skill from https://github.com/Starseed-Collective/priestess-path-carousel

Do not actually run it on the call. You already have it, and re-cloning mid-demo
wastes two minutes for no payoff. Just show them the sentence.

Do not run the demo through beforehand. The brand setup only happens on a first
run and it is the best part.

## The demo, in order

### 1. Say what it is (30 seconds, no screen)

> Every carousel I post is made this way. Same fonts, same colours, every time,
> and I never open Canva. I am going to build one in front of you.

### 2. Set the brand (2 minutes)

In Claude Code, type:

> set up my brand

It will offer you three ways. **Pick option 1, the gallery.** Six styles come
up. Pick one out loud, and say why:

> I'm picking this one, but watch, none of these are locked. This is the part
> that matters for you: it is going to be YOUR colours, not mine.

Then change something in front of them:

> make the accent colour deeper

That single moment is the whole pitch. They see it is theirs, not yours.

### 3. Make the carousel (4 minutes)

Type something like:

> Make me a 5 slide carousel about why nobody finishes their morning routine.
> Photos are in demo-photos.

Let it work out loud. It will show you the photos, write the slides, render
them, and check each one. **Do not fill the silence.** The checking step is the
thing that makes these look professional, and letting them watch it happen is
better than describing it.

### 4. Change something (1 minute)

Ask for a real edit, the kind they would ask for:

> my head is cut off on slide 2

or

> now make the story versions

Watch it fix and re-render. This is the moment people realise they are not
stuck with the first thing they get.

### 5. Show the files (30 seconds)

It will tell you the folder and open it. Show the actual JPEGs at full size.
Say the size out loud:

> These are 1080 by 1350. Ready to post. Not screenshots, not something I have
> to crop.

## If something goes wrong

**"Could not find Chrome"** — say "one second, it's installing the bit that makes
the pictures" and let Claude fix it. Takes ten seconds. Do not read the error out.

**It is slow** — talk while it works. Say what it is doing: picking the photo,
setting the crop, checking the heads are not cut off.

**Something breaks badly** - you already have finished images to fall back on.
Just ask Claude:

> open the finished demo images

Show those instead and say "here is one I made earlier." Nobody minds.

## What NOT to promise on the call

- Do not say it is free forever or that you will support it. Decide that later.
- Do not promise a date they can have it.
- If someone asks "can I have this", say "I'm working out how to hand it over,
  give me a week." That is true and it buys you room.

## The one line to end on

> The point is not that it makes pictures. The point is that it makes YOUR
> pictures, the same way, every single time, without you having to be the
> designer.
